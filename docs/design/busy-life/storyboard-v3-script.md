# A life beyond the next ring

72-second, twelve-shot storyboard. Draft for review; not a rendered film.

## 1. The impossible moment — 00–04

**Shot:** Extreme close-up · Cold open

**Action:** Wet hands steady a dog. The phone rings just out of reach. We understand the problem before anyone explains it.

**Audio:** NARRATOR: “It always rings when your hands are full.”

**Graphics:** A tiny AI Receptionist brand mark. One ring; no music yet.

**Story purpose:** Hook with a recognizable physical conflict.

## 2. She has plans, too — 04–09

**Shot:** Brief morning setup · Medium shot

**Action:** Earlier that morning: she hangs her hiking bag by the door. She has brought it so she can leave straight after work.

**Audio:** PARTNER, VOICE MESSAGE: “Still on for the lake later?”
OWNER, smiling: “That’s the plan.”

**Graphics:** A small “Earlier today” caption makes the short time shift explicit.

**Story purpose:** Plant a personal goal that the ending will pay off.

## 3. Back to the ringing phone — 09–14

**Shot:** Return to present · Eyeline match

**Action:** She glances from the phone to her soapy hands. She keeps the dog secure and returns to the wash. No exaggerated panic.

**Audio:** NARRATOR: “Someone needs you here. Someone needs you on the phone.”

**Graphics:** Second ring. Small “Incoming call” glass pill.

**Story purpose:** Make the competing demands visible; preserve her competence.

## 4. The other side of the call — 14–19

**Shot:** Reverse scene · Customer at home

**Action:** A customer waits with her terrier, Milo. She checks the time. She wants to fit a groom into her week.

**Audio:** Room tone and a short ringback; no voice-over.

**Graphics:** No warning counters or fabricated lost-revenue amounts.

**Story purpose:** Give the caller a human need, not just a sales-lead label.

## 5. A reassuring answer — 19–25

**Shot:** Return to groomer · Product reveal

**Action:** The open-eyed mascot appears beside the work scene. Ringing resolves into a calm voice; the owner continues washing.

**Audio:** AI: “Hi, I’m the AI receptionist for the grooming studio. How can I help?”

**Graphics:** Small liquid-glass surface; eyes attentive, subtle blink and speech-synced mouth.

**Story purpose:** Make the product directly resolve the opening interruption.

## 6. A specific request — 25–32

**Shot:** Customer reaction · Natural dialogue

**Action:** The caller strokes Milo and explains what she needs. We see why she called rather than watch an abstract interface.

**Audio:** CUSTOMER: “Could I book Milo for his usual groom on Thursday? After lunch would be ideal.”

**Graphics:** One short caption at a time. Returning-customer example; details established off-screen.

**Story purpose:** Show a meaningful request and preference, not a robotic yes/no exchange.

## 7. An answer that fits — 32–39

**Shot:** Product insert · Available time

**Action:** The AI presents a suitable available afternoon slot. Keep the owner visible in the background; this happens alongside her work.

**Audio:** AI: “There’s a two o’clock appointment available on Thursday. Would you like that one?”

**Graphics:** Highlight “Thursday · 2 PM”; do not make the caller navigate the interface.

**Story purpose:** Demonstrate contextual usefulness with one understandable decision.

## 8. The customer chooses — 39–44

**Shot:** Customer close-up · Relief

**Action:** She nods. Her shoulders relax. She can move on with her day, too.

**Audio:** CUSTOMER: “Two is perfect. Thank you.”

**Graphics:** The selected time settles gently into place. No narrator over dialogue.

**Story purpose:** Give the customer agency and an observable outcome.

## 9. Both customers taken care of — 44–51

**Shot:** Match cut · Towel drying

**Action:** The owner dries the dog. Beside her, the new appointment resolves into a confirmation. The two threads meet.

**Audio:** AI: “You’re all set. Milo is booked for Thursday at two. See you then.”

**Graphics:** Booking card, drawn check, three brief celebration strokes. Quiet confirmation tone.

**Story purpose:** Pay off the product promise in the same real-world scene.

## 10. It could be your business — 51–57

**Shot:** One short parallel example · Photographer

**Action:** A photographer keeps guiding a portrait customer as the same discreet answering cue appears. One new setting, no feature montage.

**Audio:** NARRATOR: “When your work needs your attention, your next customer still gets an answer.”

**Graphics:** Same mascot and glass treatment. No claim of unlimited simultaneous calls.

**Story purpose:** Broaden relevance beyond grooming without abandoning the central idea.

## 11. She keeps her plan — 57–64

**Shot:** Return to owner · Visual callback

**Action:** She collects the hiking bag we saw earlier and heads out. Show an ordinary finish to the day, not a miraculous business transformation.

**Audio:** OWNER, voice message: “On my way.”
The studio door clicks shut.

**Graphics:** Music opens up gently; no new notifications chasing her out.

**Story purpose:** Resolve the personal goal with an action rather than another slogan.

## 12. A life beyond the next ring — 64–72

**Shot:** Wide lakeside shot · Hold the ending

**Action:** The original lakeside couple and dog. She is present with them, phone put away. Let this shot breathe, then add the invitation.

**Audio:** NARRATOR: “Be there for your customers. And for yourself. Meet your AI receptionist.”

**Graphics:** “Take your day back.” → “Meet your AI receptionist.” Small connected-pilot note.

**Story purpose:** End with earned relief and one clear next step.
