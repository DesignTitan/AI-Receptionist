## Story decision

The recurring question is “Who answers when your hands are occupied?” Resolve a full dog-grooming call first, then show salon and photography bookings with shorter exchanges. A mechanic’s appointment-change request adds a different, honest outcome: a callback for the team. End with four fresh moments of uninterrupted attention. Busy means a specific task that cannot casually be dropped, not frantic acting or notification clutter. No unrelated hiking epilogue, repeated stock shots or generic industry labels.

## Reference pack — make before footage

Create one four-angle full-body sheet AND one expression close-up sheet per recurring human, using fixed wardrobe, hairstyle, age, face and proportions. Each shot references the approved sheet; never rely on a name or text description alone. Different industries deliberately have different people. Each caller has a separate identity. Keep the approved Happy Paws groomer and caller identities from prior references, rather than redesigning them.

Dog references: Samoyed has white coat, broad smiling face, black nose, no accessories. Milo uses the supplied Yorkie photograph for strand length and radial flyaway silhouette, with a tiny tongue peek and playful sock-nosing. Milo appears only with the caller. Save front and side references for both dogs; do not merge their roles.

Locations: one overhead floor plan and one wide master for each business and caller room. Label camera side, doors, phone position, work surface, windows and light direction. For grooming, explicitly show woman outside tub, dog inside. Retain a side-elevation reference proving tub rim and floor relationship. Salon tools, camera equipment and garage props get their own close references.

Mascot: reuse the selected mint fuzzy 3D character. Lock proportions, material, eyes, mouth, fur length and key light. Generate/animate a separate transparent character pass with greeting, listening, speaking and settle states. No white square, flat cutout, wobbly silhouette or new expression design between industries.

## Production method — our proposed implementation

1. Lock reference packs and the static first/last composition of each shot. Check anatomy and geography before any motion spend.
2. Record a temporary complete dialogue track first. Time actual spoken takes; the timeline below is an edit target, not permission to rush speech. If a natural take overruns, extend the shot and total runtime. Never speed up voices to force 65 seconds.
3. Generate separate Higgsfield clips with one primary action each. Request a little extra head/tail coverage. Use a model with supported image references and test one hard interaction (groomer/tub) before batching. Do not assume the model names in the X post are available.
4. For transitions, generate outgoing and incoming clips separately with matching direction, eye height and speed. Hide the cut behind real occlusion or peak blur. Assemble and retime the camera move in the editor; do not retime dialogue.
5. Composite mascot, bubbles, captions and business names afterward. Bake no text into generated footage. Use the recorded audio as timing source. Review silent and sound-on versions separately.
6. Build a rough cut with temporary sound, then approve continuity before final voice, lip sync, music and polish. Store unique shot IDs, input references, prompt, model, take and rejection reason in a manifest. Never fill a missing shot by repeating a previous clip.

## Sound and graphics rules

24 fps, 1920×1080 working master; compose important faces/action within a central safe area but plan separate vertical reframing rather than blindly cropping. No narration over call dialogue. One speaker at a time; let responses breathe approximately 0.25–0.45 seconds. Reduce machinery/music beneath voices. Use sound bridges between industries without carrying an old caller’s speech into a new business.

Mascot entrance 8 frames plus 4-frame settle, restrained secondary fur motion. Mouth follows speech energy/phonemes and closes during listening. One bubble at a time, exact spoken text, appears within 2 frames of speech start. No typewriter delay. Clear after the line with a 6-frame fade. Confirmation only after customer agreement, readable for at least 2 seconds. Bubbles must not cover faces, occupied hands or the task. No redundant “AI receptionist” headers in conversation bubbles. Captions remain independently available; no duplicate captions plus full-size bubble in the same region.

## Acceptance checklist — reject before assembly

- Every shot has one unique source take and a physical action; no duplicated footage, reversed replay or slow zoom standing in for missing footage.
- Reference comparison verifies face, outfit, animal, phone hand/ear, room geometry, lighting, tool contact and wetness stage.
- Groomer stays outside tub in all shots; feet/floor proof exists. No spare hands, merged tools, floating props, stretched dog limbs or person/dog intersections.
- The offer, agreement and outcome match by business, service, day and time. A requested change is never shown as completed.
- Every transition lands in a readable new environment before dialogue begins. No accidental camera-direction reversal or illegible text during blur.
- Lip sync and overlays checked against audio frame by frame. No dead air caused by waiting for graphics.
- Watch once at normal speed without sound, once with sound and once on a phone. If the action or speaker cannot be identified, simplify the shot before adding explanatory copy.
