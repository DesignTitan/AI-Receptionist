# Luxury hero revision — 8 September 2026

Bubs rejected the first concepts as generic, cheesy AI imagery. This revision preserves four ideas but rebuilds the visual language around luxurious, elite, futuristic precision. The 1:1 format stays; each asset is one large image instead of a four-panel sheet.

## Reusable visual grammar

- Monumental photographic subjects and generous negative space.
- Near-black, titanium grey and cool white; restrained middle tones and sharp white light.
- Real material detail, strict alignment, coherent perspective and gravity.
- Large sans-serif typography and small supporting labels, with no generated interface text.
- One clear subject and one product message at a time.

## Reference boundaries

- Reference: the official SpaceX Starship page, inspected in the browser on 8 September 2026: https://www.spacex.com/vehicles/starship.
- Target: recognizable influence in scale, photographic confidence and restraint (about 50% in the skill's descriptive similarity dial, not a measured score).
- Original subjects, arrangement, product story and typography. No SpaceX marks, slogan, spacecraft, people, exact photographic composition or copied media.

## Four studies

1. Precision: ordinary graphite smartphone photographed as a monumental instrument.
2. Architecture: a full-scale metal-and-white reception atrium; the miniature concept becomes a real architectural space.
3. Interface: a restrained aligned stack of software planes, with exact product explanation provided separately in HTML.
4. Evolution: three ordinary telephone generations in one disciplined photographic progression. Suggested motion uses precise match cuts rather than melting forms.

The eventual evolution sequence should spend roughly 20% on historical devices, then show one online booking, the outbound AI confirmation call and the owner's call record. The product is software; no new AI hardware or inbound answering is implied. Reschedule requests go to staff.

These are new visual anchors and proposed stories, not rendered video. Keep the original comparison intact for reference. Review the new art direction before connected motion production. Final product UI, headings and controls must be readable web elements; mobile and reduced-motion composition remain required.

## Value-first steering from Bubs

Bubs asked to use Starlink's approach to showing what a service lets people do: travel, be away, take a break. The human benefit must lead. A beautiful device photograph alone is insufficient.

- With a customer: give the person in front of you attention while AI handles appointment-confirmation calls.
- Away from the desk: customers can choose available appointments online without waiting for the owner to return.
- Taking a break: reduce the manual work of chasing confirmations.
- Taking time off: customers can still book online, and booking/call records stay organized for the owner to review. Requests needing a person still require follow-up; do not imply customer team accounts.

Opening proposal: **Step away. Bookings keep moving.** Explain immediately: **Your customers book online. AI calls them to confirm. You see the outcome.** A fifth supporting photograph shows an owner taking a real break on a quiet coastal terrace, with the phone face down. This human benefit image leads into the four technical storytelling options.

The service benefit appears immediately over large travel photography, followed by specific travel, camping and boating scenarios. Borrow that narrative structure with original owner situations and imagery. Do not imply global call coverage, inbound answering, guaranteed bookings, unlimited calls, or complete unattended business operation. Avoid the absolute claim “never miss another booking.”
