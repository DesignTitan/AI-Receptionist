# AI Receptionist — photography and video brief

Updated 9 September 2026. Production direction from Bubs's existing photo/video requests and renewed Starlink reference. This is the shot brief for review before producing motion, not a record of completed media or an activated phone service.

## The idea

**Give owners more time for the person, work or life in front of them, while appointments keep moving.**

The audience is the owner of an appointment business, starting with salons and similar services. A viewer should immediately recognize their own day: a customer in the chair, a busy reception area, a short errand, lunch, the end of a shift, or time off.

The main visual story is the owner getting that time back. Show the booking work alongside it so the benefit has a clear cause. Every scene must answer: what can this owner do, what appointment task does AI Receptionist handle, and where can the owner see the result?

## What the Starlink reference teaches

Inspected the official [Starlink Roam page](https://www.starlink.com/roam) in the browser on 9 September 2026. Its opening uses a broad natural setting; the scenario section shows a vehicle travelling, a campsite and a video call on a boat. The situations make the service relevant before the visitor needs to understand its technology.

Our interpretation: lead with a recognizable situation, connect it to one practical benefit, then show the product doing the work. Borrow the scale, visual restraint and clarity. Use original business situations and media. This research did not establish that Starlink uses a scroll-scrubbed video implementation; our scroll sequence is our own proposed treatment.

## Finish with proof

End on one clear customer appointment in the owner dashboard: the saved time, the call outcome and any request needing a person. Show the owner glancing at the result and returning to their day. This is a product capture plus a human scene, not another speculative photograph of floating software.

Keep the mechanics readable in HTML or an actual interface capture. Generated media supplies the people, space and camera movement. It must not invent appointment details, notification delivery, prices or confirmation states.

For the incoming phone story, the complete sequence is:

**Customer calls → owner’s answering choice → AI checks a real slot → caller agrees → appointment is saved → owner sees the record.**

For the current online/outgoing story:

**Customer books online → AI calls to confirm if enabled → owner sees the booking and call outcome.**

Do not combine these into an unexplained montage. The visitor should be able to tell which flow they are watching.

It should occupy no more than roughly one fifth of that explanation; human benefit and product evidence get the rest. ## Visual and production direction

- Aim for a premium documentary campaign: real-looking environments, natural light, believable skin, hair, tools and working posture; no posing at the viewer, waxy faces, theatrical panic or stock-photo headset smiles.
- Use one consistent fictional owner/business across work scenes and appropriate continuity through the time-off scenes. Tanaz is the pilot context, not permission to invent photographs or testimonials of its real staff.
- Luxury comes from composition, space, attention and time. Keep the website framing clean and dark, with large imagery and sparse white typography. The salon itself should feel usable and welcoming, not like a spacecraft interior.
- Use Higgsfield as requested. Confirm the current still-image and image-to-video models before production. The earlier interpretation of “Canto 2.5” as Cinema Studio Image 2.5 was not a verified model alias. Judge actual output quality; no model choice guarantees perfect realism.
- Review stills in the requested 1:1 comparison format. Plan separate wide desktop and portrait mobile compositions for final placement. Do not assume every square frame can crop successfully into both.
- Motion should come from the camera and ordinary human/environmental movement. Avoid morphing people, melting phones, floating objects, ornamental waveforms and generated UI lettering.

## Deliverables and review order

1. **This brief:** recovered scenarios, their benefit and the proof each requires.
2. **First still review:** focused-client and busy-reception scenes alongside the already approved opening. Check realism and continuity before commissioning the full set.
3. **Complete keyframes:** the six scenario photographs, using the approved opening as the campaign anchor. Each has its planned mobile composition and fallback still.
4. **Short motion test:** one approved human scene and one product sequence. Review pacing and clarity before generating all clips.
5. **Final local page assembly:** use approved photos/video and accurate product captures, then check desktop/mobile, reduced motion and load performance before publishing.

## Acceptance checks

- Within five seconds, a viewer can identify the business problem and explain why the owner has more time.
- Every photo shows a requested situation; every adjacent product state explains how appointments are handled.
- A viewer can distinguish online booking, outgoing confirmation and incoming AI answering.
- Text remains readable without sound or motion. The owner/caller task stays understandable on mobile.
- Incoming claims use real pilot evidence before they are presented as a working live service. Neither pilot phone is connected yet. External booking software sync and email delivery also require verification.
- No absolute promises of never missing a booking, unlimited calling or complete unattended operation. Prefer specific demonstrated results over guarantees.
