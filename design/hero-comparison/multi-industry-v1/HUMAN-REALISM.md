## Research and application

- [Adobe: Generate realistic photos](https://helpx.adobe.com/ca/firefly/how-to/generate-realistic-photos.html) recommends combining a portrait reference with explicit pose, framing, lighting and material detail. Apply this by establishing one close-up identity portrait before the crowded sheet.
- [Adobe Firefly prompting guide](https://community.adobe.com/p/firefly-image-guide) includes portrait examples specifying pores, freckles, skin texture and lighting. Use selective, natural detail rather than generic quality adjectives.
- [Higgsfield skin-enhancement guide](https://higgsfield.ai/blog/The-Skin-Enhancer-How-to-Retouch-Pictures-with-AI) identifies blur-based smoothing as a cause of artificial faces. Its enhancement claims are vendor claims, not a guarantee; do not automatically run a beautifying or face-restoration pass.

The following are our art-direction choices, informed by those references. They are not guaranteed model settings and must be checked visually.

## Required identity description for every realistic person

1. Apparent age and individual face shape; mild natural asymmetry. Do not default everyone to a youthful model face.
2. Complexion depth and undertone, with restrained variation around cheeks, nose and under-eyes. Preserve darker skin values under lighting; do not brighten the complexion to expose texture. Never infer ethnicity from skin colour.
3. Age-appropriate lines and their location: outer-eye creases that deepen when smiling, subtle forehead folds when brows lift, natural smile folds. Not identical engraved wrinkles in every expression.
4. Small, nonuniform pores where visible at the chosen framing; subtle peach fuzz, lip texture and normal skin sheen. No uniform pore stamp, waxy cheeks or exaggerated gritty skin.
5. Specific iris colour, natural sclera, normal pupil size, fine lashes and brows. Catchlights must follow the light source, not become identical luminous dots.
6. Hair base colour, length, part, strand texture, hairline, density, flyaways and any restrained greying. Keep the same bun or part between views.
7. Hands, neck and ears must match the face's age and complexion. Include normal knuckle folds and nail detail in close views.
8. Record any distinctive freckle or mole once in the master. Preserve its position in subsequent shots; do not invent different marks in each cell.

## Photograph direction

- An unretouched working-person portrait, with a relaxed expression and ordinary grooming, not a glossy beauty campaign.
- One broad window-like light from the side, gentle fill, readable shadows and restrained highlights. Avoid flattening all facial planes with frontal fill.
- Head-and-shoulders framing, an 85mm-equivalent portrait perspective and enough depth of field to keep both eyes and cheeks readable. Camera words are visual instructions, not evidence of real capture settings.
- Neutral colour for the reference portrait. Apply scene lighting later without changing the person's complexion or iris colour.
- Do not rely on “8K,” “flawless,” “perfect skin,” “HDR” or “ultra-sharp” as realism instructions. Grain cannot repair incorrect anatomy or waxy skin.
- Facial texture must become less visible in wider shots. Do not preserve macro-sized pores on a tiny full-body figure.

## Generation sequence and review

1. Generate one close-up master per person using the existing hosted identity reference. Change photographic treatment, not the person.
2. Inspect face, eyes, hairline, neck and hands at full size and at the actual storyboard display size. Reject excessive smoothing, crunchy texture, glassy eyes, inconsistent skin values or sculpted hair clumps.
3. Use the successful master as the shared reference for every angle, expression and pose. Keep wardrobe and permanent features fixed.
4. Check every sheet panel for drift; a dense sheet is not proof of identity consistency. Reference portraits carry the identity, while sheets communicate variations.
5. Produce individual scene frames with the approved cast and match lighting, exposure, wetness, props and action across cuts.
